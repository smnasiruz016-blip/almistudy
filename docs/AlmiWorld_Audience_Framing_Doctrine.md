# AlmiWorld Audience-Framing Doctrine

**Status:** Locked in Day 6 session (2026-05-10) after two doctrine-drift
corrections by founder.

## Core rule

AlmiWorld products serve **EVERYONE**, not specifically migrants.

## What this means in practice

### Hard rule — never use these framings

In commands, copy, product strategy, or anywhere else:

- ❌ "migrant audience"
- ❌ "migrant users"
- ❌ "migrant workers"
- ❌ "for migrants" as audience-defining phrase
- ❌ "migrant-focused" as product positioning

### Correct framings to use instead

- ✓ "users"
- ✓ "anyone seeking [X]"
- ✓ "global mobile-first audience"
- ✓ "people researching universities" / "people building CVs" / etc.
- ✓ "users applying internationally" (when destination-country context is relevant)

## Why this matters

### The founder context

The founder (Nasir, Pakistani migrant in Reykjavík) built AlmiWorld
because he witnessed migrant exploitation by recruitment agencies,
education consultants, and credential-evaluation predators. The
doctrine was forged by this lived experience.

### But the products are universal

The honest-information directories AlmiWorld builds — verified
university accreditation, public salary data, ad-free job listings,
free CV templates — benefit ANYONE, not just migrants:

- A Pakistani student in Lahore researching UK universities for
  postgraduate study uses AlmiStudy the same way a British student
  researching US universities does.
- An Indian software engineer in Bangalore building a CV for a
  remote-first European role uses AlmiCV the same way a German
  engineer building a CV for a Berlin job does.
- A Filipino nurse researching salary expectations in Saudi Arabia
  uses AlmiSalary the same way a Saudi nurse researching salary
  expectations in the UAE does.

### Migrants are a focus segment, not the audience definition

It's fair to say AlmiWorld's doctrine choices (no diploma mills,
no recruitment-agency upsells, accreditation honesty, free access)
were SHAPED by understanding migrant exploitation. Those choices
HAPPEN to serve everyone, but the design intent came from witnessing
predation against migrants specifically.

Saying "migrants are an important focus segment" is accurate.
Saying "AlmiWorld is for migrants" is inaccurate audience-narrowing
that fails everyone else who benefits.

## Application to product copy

### AlmiStudy

- ✓ "A directory. We point you to verified-accreditation universities."
- ✓ "Free to browse. No account required, no CV upload, no applications brokered."
- ❌ "Helping migrants find safe universities"
- ❌ "For students from developing countries"

### AlmiJob

- ✓ "Job listings without recruitment-agency fees"
- ❌ "Jobs for migrants seeking opportunities abroad"

### AlmiCV

- ✓ "Free CV templates that work"
- ❌ "CVs for migrant workers"

### AlmiSalary

- ✓ "Honest salary data, free to access"
- ❌ "Salary data for migrants negotiating jobs"

## When migrant context IS appropriate

The migrant audience CAN be discussed in contexts where it's
factually relevant:

- Research/strategy docs explaining WHY doctrine choices were made
  (referencing the predation patterns that informed the doctrine)
- Internal positioning when discussing why we don't take affiliate
  fees from listed institutions
- Specific feature decisions like `recognitionByDestination[]` for
  AlmiStudy where credential portability is a real user need
- Discussions of specific user pain points that disproportionately
  affect migrants (e.g., credential recognition friction)

The line: discuss migrant context where it INFORMS the work without
NARROWING the audience to migrants in user-facing copy or product
positioning.

## Future-session checklist

Before writing any user-facing copy, product positioning text, or
strategic framing, verify against this doctrine:

1. Am I using "migrant" as the audience definition? → Stop and rephrase.
2. Am I narrowing to migrants when the product serves everyone? → Broaden.
3. Am I discussing migrant context as informing doctrine choices? → Acceptable.
4. Does this copy work equally well for a student in London as for
   a student in Karachi? → If yes, doctrine-aligned. If no, fix it.
